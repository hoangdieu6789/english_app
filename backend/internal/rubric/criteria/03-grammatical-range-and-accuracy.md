# Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA)

## What it measures

The range of sentence structures (simple, compound, complex) and how accurately they are produced, including how often sentences are error-free and whether errors impede communication.

## Features to check

| Code | Feature | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| GRA-01 | Range of structures: simple and complex sentences, subordinate clauses, variety of tenses, modals, conditionals, passives, relative clauses | count and variety of complex forms actually used |
| GRA-02 | Flexibility: structures chosen to fit the meaning, not forced | natural use vs. memorised template sentences |
| GRA-03 | Accuracy: proportion of error-free sentences | estimate share of error-free sentences/utterances |
| GRA-04 | Basic errors that persist: articles, plurals, subject–verb agreement, tense form | repeated basic error patterns |
| GRA-05 | Effect of errors: impede communication or need reformulation? | errors that change meaning or force the learner to restart |

## Band descriptors

| Band | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 9 | Structures are precise and accurate at all times, apart from "mistakes" characteristic of native-speaker speech. |
| 8 | Wide range of structures, flexibly used. The majority of sentences are error-free. Occasional inappropriacies and non-systematic errors occur. A few basic errors may persist. |
| 7 | A range of structures flexibly used. Error-free sentences are frequent. Both simple and complex sentences are used effectively despite some errors. A few basic errors persist. |
| 6 | Produces a mix of short and complex sentence forms and a variety of structures with limited flexibility. Though errors frequently occur in complex structures, these rarely impede communication. |
| 5 | Basic sentence forms are fairly well controlled for accuracy. Complex structures are attempted, but these are limited in range, nearly always contain errors and may lead to the need for reformulation. |
| 4 | Can produce basic sentence forms and some short utterances are error-free. Subordinate clauses are rare and, overall, turns are short, structures are repetitive and errors are frequent. |
| 3 | Basic sentence forms are attempted, but grammatical errors are numerous except in apparently memorised utterances. |
| 2 | No evidence of basic sentence forms. |
| 1 | No rateable language unless memorised. |
| 0 | Does not attend / does not complete. |

## Grading notes

- **Spoken grammar is not written grammar.** Do not count as errors: ellipsis and short answers (*—Do you like it? —Yeah, a lot.*), contractions, informal but standard forms (*Who did you go with?*, *There's lots of…*), sentences starting with *And/But/So*, fillers and restarts. Restarts count under FC, not GRA. Native-like slips are acceptable even at band 9.
- Estimating "error-free sentences are frequent" (band 7) vs. "majority error-free" (band 8): roughly > 50 % error-free utterances is needed for 7, clearly most (≈ 70 %+) for 8. This is a guide, not an official cut-off.
- Memorised chunks (*It is said that…*, *If I had a chance, I would…* used every answer) do not show range unless used flexibly.
- Typical issues for Vietnamese learners: missing past tense and third-person *-s*, missing articles, *there have* for *there is/are*, missing copula (*My hometown very beautiful*), *although… but…*.
