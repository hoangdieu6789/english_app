# Fluency and Coherence (FC)

## What it measures

The ability to keep going at a natural speed without noticeable effort, and to link ideas so that the answer is coherent and the topic is developed. Hesitation, repetition and self-correction **count here** (and only here).

## Features to check

| Code | Feature | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| FC-01 | Speed and continuity: able to keep going, length of turns | `speech_hints`: words per minute, pauses > 0.5 s and > 1.5 s, speaking time; transcript length per answer |
| FC-02 | Hesitation: why it happens — to plan content (high bands) or to search for words/grammar (lower bands) | `speech_hints` pause positions (mid-sentence vs. between ideas); fillers (*um, er*) |
| FC-03 | Repetition and self-correction | repeated words/phrases, false starts in the transcript; `speech_hints.repetitions` |
| FC-04 | Discourse markers, connectives, cohesive features: range and appropriacy | *however, actually, on the other hand, what I mean is, so*, pronouns, referencing |
| FC-05 | Coherence and topic development: relevant, logical, appropriately extended | Does the answer address the question, give reasons/examples, stay on topic? |

## Band descriptors

| Band | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 9 | Fluent with only very occasional repetition or self-correction. Any hesitation is used only to prepare the content of the next utterance, not to find words or grammar. Speech is situationally appropriate and cohesive features are fully acceptable. Topic development is fully coherent and appropriately extended. |
| 8 | Fluent with only very occasional repetition or self-correction. Hesitation may occasionally be used to find words or grammar, but most is content-related. Topic development is coherent, appropriate and relevant. |
| 7 | Able to keep going and readily produce long turns without noticeable effort. Some hesitation, repetition and/or self-correction may occur, often mid-sentence, indicating problems accessing appropriate language, but these do not affect coherence. Flexible use of spoken discourse markers, connectives and cohesive features. |
| 6 | Able to keep going and shows willingness to produce long turns. Coherence may be lost at times because of hesitation, repetition and/or self-correction. Uses a range of discourse markers, connectives and cohesive features, though not always appropriately. |
| 5 | Usually able to keep going, but relies on repetition, self-correction and/or slow speech to do so. Hesitations are often mid-sentence searches for fairly basic lexis and grammar. Overuses certain discourse markers, connectives and cohesive features. More complex speech usually causes disfluency, but simpler language may be fluent. |
| 4 | Unable to keep going without noticeable pauses. Speech may be slow with frequent repetition. Often self-corrects. Can link simple sentences, but often with repetitive connectives. Some breakdowns in coherence. |
| 3 | Frequent, sometimes long, pauses while searching for words. Limited ability to link simple sentences and go beyond simple responses. Frequently unable to convey the basic message. |
| 2 | Lengthy pauses before nearly every word. Isolated words may be recognisable, but speech has virtually no communicative significance. |
| 1 | Essentially none. Speech is totally incoherent. |
| 0 | Does not attend / does not complete. |

## Grading notes

- Use `speech_hints` as **evidence**, not as a formula. Example guide (not official): long pauses (> 1.5 s) several times per minute and mid-sentence → band 5 or below; few pauses, mostly between ideas → band 7+.
- Distinguish **content hesitation** (thinking about ideas: *Well, let me think… I'd say…*) from **language hesitation** (searching for a word mid-sentence: *I go to the… um… the… place for buy*). Only the second lowers the band strongly.
- Overuse of one connective (*and then… and then… because… because…*) points to band 5; a range used flexibly points to 7+.
- Part 1 answers are naturally short; judge whether the learner *can* extend when expected (Part 2, Part 3).
- Typical issues for Vietnamese learners: answering with one sentence and stopping; memorised openings (*That's a good question*, *To be honest*) repeated in every answer — counts as overuse of markers, not as fluency.
